Access Collins / DesignMyNight — the hidden UK gem

DesignMyNight is the consumer discovery platform (3.9M monthly users, 500k newsletter subscribers). Access Collins is the backend booking system used by those venues. It powers pubs, bars, restaurants, and experience venues across the UK. It's a Google AI Mode UK partner, integrates with 70+ software partners, and is already connected to ResDiary for cross-platform bookings. The £2/cover from the DMN platform is cheap for the restaurants, suggesting a price-sensitive base — but the event and group booking use case is high-value for voice AI (large party confirmations, pre-order calls).

Eveve — the overlooked UK player with US ambitions

Founded 2007 in the UK, Eveve claims to be the third largest supplier in the UK by online reservation volume. Critically, its largest market has now shifted to the US — processing over 4 million online diners annually there. 2,000+ restaurants across 24 countries. Proprietary table management (TELOS). Has API integrations with TripAdvisor and Google. No published pricing — custom quotes only. This is worth a direct conversation. If Eveve's US footprint is real, a PolyAI integration could unlock a meaningful US long-tail market PolyAI hasn't addressed.

Platform-first voice AI — the growing threat

Every major booking platform is building its own voice AI, closing the window for third-party players.

SevenRooms Voice AI — live, but no named customers yet

SevenRooms built first-party voice AI powered by their full CRM (guest spend history, preferences, visit frequency). Same-day go-live, dozens of voice/accent options. Product page is live but zero published case studies or CSAT — it's too new. PolyAI's window: cross-platform chains can't use SR Voice AI at their non-SR venues. No evidence of any publicly named restaurant using it.

Yelp Host — $99–149/mo, built in-house, Oct 2025

Yelp built proprietary voice AI (not white-labelled) using LLMs + Yelp's own restaurant data. 24/7 reservation management, real-time wait times, SMS follow-up, spam filtering. Only works with Yelp Guest Manager. US + Canada. No CSAT published. At $99–149/mo it effectively undercuts every competitor for Yelp-native customers.

OpenTable — marketplace model, no first-party voice AI yet

OpenTable is the only major platform still running a third-party voice AI marketplace (14 providers including PolyAI). OpenTable Media launched Feb 2026 (advertising product) — not voice AI. Watch closely: if OpenTable builds first-party, PolyAI's primary distribution channel changes overnight. For now, this is the most important window to maximise.

ResDiary — no voice AI at all. The biggest opportunity.

ResPhone is basic call routing, not AI. ResDiary Evo adds AI for no-show prediction and VIP recognition, but no voice answering. 9,000+ venues, UK/IE/AU, solid REST API. One integration unlocks three markets with zero voice AI competition. This window will close once The Access Group (ResDiary's parent) decides to build it themselves.

Platform customer segments

Who each platform targets, where PolyAI's ~$300/mo price point fits — and crucially, which platforms serve high-end restaurants most likely to want and afford voice AI.

Why high-end restaurants are PolyAI's best target customers

The more upscale the restaurant, the stronger the case for voice AI on three dimensions: (1) Volume — fine dining and upscale venues receive significantly more inbound calls (reservations, dietary queries, special occasion planning, private dining enquiries) than casual dining. (2) Affordability — a restaurant charging £100+ per head per person can easily justify £300/mo for voice AI; a café charging £12 for lunch cannot. (3) Brand stakes — a missed call or robotic-sounding AI damages a luxury restaurant's reputation far more than it damages a casual chain's. PolyAI's human-like voice quality is most valuable precisely where brand experience matters most.

The sweet spot — where PolyAI wins

PolyAI's ideal restaurant customer is on SevenRooms, Resy, or the upscale tier of OpenTable — venues charging £60–250+ per cover, where a missed call means losing a £400+ table, and where voice quality reflects directly on the brand. These restaurants already spend hundreds per month on booking software, are used to enterprise-grade tools, and have the budget. The further down the spend-per-cover scale you go, the harder it is to justify £300/mo and the less PolyAI's human-like voice quality provides differentiation over cheaper alternatives.
